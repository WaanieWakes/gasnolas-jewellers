### Scrap / buy-back reference (what gold buyers pay in SA)

The Gold Avenue (Sandton) publishes buy-back rates — useful as a floor value for old gold and as a sanity check that your quoted material cost is above scrap value:
- 9ct: **≈ R 730/g** (vs spot R 828.67 → ~88% of spot)
- 18ct: **≈ R 1,460/g** (vs spot R 1,657.34 → ~88% of spot)

Rule of thumb: a fair buy-back is **85–90% of spot**; retail material cost should never be below buy-back.

## 2. SA jeweller benchmarks

**Important context:** Tanishq and Malabar are **Indian retail chains**; they do not have a mainstream retail footprint in South Africa. They are useful only as a *reference for making-charge % and pricing discipline*, not as local competitors. In SA the relevant comparators are:

| Segment | Examples | Positioning | Typical purity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Luxury / designer | Shimansky (Cape Town), Miller Gold & Co | Brand premium, diamond/tanzanite focus, high making % | 18k, platinum |
| Fine custom / made-to-order | Prodiam (cutting house), Platgold, Arias Jewellery Palace | Bespoke, hand-set stones, by-appointment | 18k, 14k |
| Mid-market retail | Local mall jewellers, chain stores | Machine-made, standard designs, volume | 9k, 14k, 18k |
| Independent / home-based makers | Etsy Cape Town makers, Instagram jewellers, goldsmiths | Custom, low overhead, direct-to-client | 9k, 18k, silver |
| Scrap & exchange | The Gold Avenue, cash-for-gold buyers | Buy-back / melt value | all purities |

**Benchmarks from research:**
- Indian chain making charges (Tanishq/Malabar/Kalyan) run **~6% to 25% of gold value** depending on design complexity and machine vs hand work, and have surged with gold prices (Tanishq reported moving from ~18% to ~25% over a year). Treat **10–20% of gold value** as the "chain retail" making-charge band.
- Saudi/Asian retail reference: **5–25% of gold value** for making charges, or a **fixed R/gram** (e.g. R 8–R 60/g in SAR terms; scale to ZAR).
- Scrap buy-back in SA: **~88% of spot** (Gold Avenue).
- Independent/handmade makers (global norm): **2× to 4× cost** markup on materials + labour (metalsmith society / independent jeweler guides).

**What this means for Dad (home-based, low overhead):** He can price **below chain retail** (no mall rent, no brand premium) but **above scrap + a thin margin**. A realistic target is **cost + 30–60% on metal, plus a clear per-gram making charge, plus stone cost + 40–80%**. This undercuts mall jewellers while paying properly for skill.

## 3. Making-charge norms in SA (R/gram by complexity)

No SA jeweller publishes a universal R/gram tariff publicly; charges are quoted per piece. The table below is a **recommended working tariff for Dad**, built from global norms (fixed-rate-per-gram and % of gold value) adapted to SA labour costs and low overhead. Label quotes clearly ("making charge = labour + design + finishing").

| Complexity | Description | R / gram (9k) | R / gram (18k) | % of gold value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple / machine** | Plain band, simple chain, basic setting, minimal finishing | R 120 – R 200 | R 180 – R 300 | 8 – 12% |
| **Standard** | Engagement ring, pendant with setting, engraved band | R 200 – R 350 | R 300 – R 500 | 12 – 18% |
| **Complex / hand** | Hand-forged, filigree, multi-stone, custom design, resizing | R 350 – R 600 | R 500 – R 900 | 18 – 25% |
| **Bespoke / artistic** | One-off design, CAD + hand finish, heirloom repair/restoration | R 600 – R 1,200+ | R 900 – R 1,800+ | 25 – 40% |

**Silver making charges** (lower metal cost, similar labour): R 60 – R 250/g depending on complexity; or 15–30% of silver value.

**Additional line items to always charge:**
- Design/consultation time: R 250 – R 600/hour (or a flat R 300–R 800 design fee, refundable against the order).
- Stone setting: R 80 – R 250 per stone (simple claw) up to R 400–R 800 (pavé/micro).
- Engraving: R 150 – R 400.
- Polishing/refinishing: R 100 – R 300.
- Rhodium plating (white gold): R 250 – R 600 per piece.
- Hallmarking/assay (if used): cost + 10%.

## 4. Pricing formulas for custom pieces

### Core formula (cost-plus, recommended default)

```
Retail price = (Gold weight (g) × Spot rate for purity (R/g))
            + (Making charge (R/g × weight) OR % of gold value)
            + (Stone cost × stone markup)
            + (Consumables & overhead allowance)
            + (Profit margin %)
```

Worked example — 18k custom ring, 4.2 g, simple solitaire with a 0.5 ct CZ (cost R 120):

| Line | Calc | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Gold material | 4.2 g × R 1,657.34 | R 6,960.83 |
| Making charge (standard, 18k) | 4.2 g × R 400 | R 1,680.00 |
| Stone | R 120 × 1.6 | R 192.00 |
| Consumables/overhead | 5% of material+making | R 432.04 |
| Subtotal cost | | R 9,264.87 |
| Profit margin (35%) | × 1.35 | **R 12,507.57** |
| Round to retail | | **R 12,500** |

### Alternative formulas

**A. Cost-plus (recommended for Dad):** `Cost × (1 + margin%)`. Simple, defensible, easy to re-quote when gold moves. Use **margin 30–50%** on metal+making, **40–80%** on stones.

**B. Value-based:** Price to what the piece is *worth to the client* (sentimental, design uniqueness, brand of maker) rather than cost. Use for one-off bespoke and heirloom work; can yield 2×–5× cost. Requires confidence and a portfolio.

**C. Competitive:** Anchor to local comparators (mall jewellers' R/gram, Etsy independents). Undercut chains by 10–20% while staying 15–30% above scrap+cost. Good for standard designs; risky for bespoke.

**D. Hybrid (best practice):** Cost-plus floor + competitive ceiling + value-based uplift for bespoke. Never price below cost-plus floor.

### Stone pricing
- Cost stones (CZ, synthetic): cost × 1.5–2.0.
- Natural diamonds/gemstones: cost × 1.3–1.6 (lower %, higher absolute) plus certification/setting.
- Always quote stones **separately** from metal so the client sees the split and can swap.

### Weight & wastage
- Quote on **finished weight** plus a **2–4% wastage allowance** on metal (filing, polishing, sprue). Disclose or absorb into making charge; be consistent.

## 5. Markup strategies

| Strategy | When to use | Typical markup | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus | Standard custom, repairs, most of Dad's work | 30–50% on metal+making; 40–80% on stones | Predictable, easy to defend |
| Value-based | Bespoke, heirloom, signature designs | 2×–5× cost | Needs portfolio & confidence |
| Competitive | Plain bands, chains, commodity items | 10–20% below chains | Race to bottom |
| Tiered (recommended) | Mix of stock + custom | Stock 20–30%, custom 40–60%, bespoke 60%+ | Best overall |

**Markup guidance for a home-based maker:**
- Don't compete on commodity gold price — you can't beat chain volume buying. Compete on **craft, customisation, and service**.
- Always keep a **minimum 25% margin** even on "favours" for friends; below that you're subsidising.
- Re-price quotes if gold moves **> 2%** between quote and acceptance.
- Round retail prices to clean numbers (R 12,500 not R 12,507.57).

## 6. Currency considerations (ZAR volatility, hedging)

- **ZAR is volatile** (≈ R 16.5/USD, swings of 5–10% over months). Since gold is priced in USD, a weak rand raises local gold cost even if USD gold is flat — and vice versa.
- **Impact on Dad:** material cost can shift between quoting and buying gold. If he quotes in ZAR and buys gold later, a rand weakening squeezes margin.
- **Hedging options for a small maker:**
  1. **Quote validity short (24–48 h)** with a re-quote clause if gold moves > 2%.
  2. **Buy metal in advance** when rand is strong; hold physical gold (Krugerrands) as a mini-hedge — SA's most liquid gold instrument.
  3. **Price in USD-equivalent + ZAR conversion** for export/foreign clients; for local clients always quote ZAR.
  4. **Add a currency buffer** of 3–5% into the making charge or overhead line.
  5. **Keep gold stock low** (buy per job) to avoid holding depreciating metal; but buy in larger batches when rand is strong to lock cost.
  6. **Track the rate weekly** (goldprice.org, GoldBroker) and adjust the price list monthly.
- **Tax/compliance:** keep invoices in ZAR; if selling across borders, declare properly. (Not tax advice — consult a SA tax practitioner.)

## 7. How to quote custom work (per gram + making + stones)

**Quote structure (always itemised):**

```
QUOTATION — [Client] — [Date] — Valid 48 h
1. Metal: [purity] × [weight g] × spot rate R/g          = R xxxx
2. Making charge: [complexity] × weight g (or % of metal)  = R xxxx
3. Stones: [list] cost × markup                           = R xxxx
4. Setting labour: [n stones × R/stone]                    = R xxxx
5. Extras: engraving / plating / design fee                = R xxxx
6. Subtotal                                                = R xxxx
7. Wastage allowance (2–4% of metal)                       = R xxxx
8. TOTAL (excl. VAT if applicable)                         = R xxxx
Deposit required: 50% (R xxxx) to start; balance on collection.
Lead time: X weeks. Gold price re-quoted if spot moves > 2%.
```

**Quoting rules:**
- Always quote **weight + purity + spot rate** so the client sees the metal is market-linked.
- Separate metal, making, and stones — never one lump sum.
- Take **50% deposit** before buying metal; metal is non-returnable.
- Note **lead time** and that final weight may vary ±0.2 g (adjust invoice at collection).
- For repairs/restoration: quote a **flat labour fee + materials** (no metal markup if client supplies gold).
- Keep a **written record** (photo of piece + weight + purity) for disputes.
